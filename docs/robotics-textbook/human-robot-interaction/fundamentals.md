# Fundamentals of Human-Robot Interaction

## What Makes HRI Different?

Human-robot interaction differs from human-computer interaction in several key ways:

**Physical Presence**: Robots exist in the same physical space as humans, creating opportunities for spatial interaction and requiring safety considerations.

**Embodied Communication**: Robots can communicate through movement, posture, and physical actions, not just visual displays or audio.

**Social Expectations**: Humans often anthropomorphize robots, expecting social behaviors even from simple machines.

**Dynamic Environment**: Both human and robot may be moving, creating a constantly changing interaction context.

## Key Principles of HRI

**Predictability**: Humans should be able to predict robot behavior based on its actions and communication.

**Transparency**: The robot should make its intentions and state clear to human users.

**Appropriateness**: Robot behavior should be appropriate for the context and cultural setting.

**Initiative**: Balance between robot initiative and human control based on the task and situation.

## Interaction Modalities

Robots can interact with humans through multiple channels:

**Verbal Communication**: Speech recognition and synthesis for natural language interaction.

**Non-verbal Communication**: Gestures, facial expressions, posture, and proxemics (spatial relationships).

**Physical Interaction**: Direct physical contact, including haptic feedback and collaborative manipulation.

**Visual Communication**: Displays, lights, and visual feedback to convey state and intentions.
