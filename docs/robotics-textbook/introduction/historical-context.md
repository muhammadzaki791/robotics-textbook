# Historical Context of Robotics

The dream of creating artificial beings that move and act like humans dates back thousands of years, but modern robotics has a more recent history.

## Early Mechanical Automata

The concept of artificial mechanical beings isn't new. Ancient civilizations created mechanical devices that mimicked life:

- **Ancient Greece**: Hephaestus, the god of craftsmanship, was said to have created golden robots to serve him
- **1st Century CE**: Hero of Alexandria created mechanical theaters and automated devices
- **18th Century**: Pierre Jaquet-Droz built intricate mechanical dolls that could write, draw, and play music

## The Birth of Modern Robotics

The modern era of robotics began in the 20th century:

- **1921**: Karel Čapek's play "R.U.R." (Rossum's Universal Robots) introduced the word "robot" to the world
- **1950s**: George Devol created the first programmable robot, Unimate, which began work in a General Motors factory in 1961
- **1960s**: The Stanford Arm and MIT's "Handy" robot laid groundwork for manipulation research

## Humanoid Robotics Emerges

The development of humanoid robots gained momentum in the late 20th century:

- **1970s**: WABOT-1 at Waseda University in Japan was one of the first complete humanoid robots
- **1980s**: Honda began development of humanoid walking robots
- **1997**: Honda's P2 was one of the first humanoid robots to demonstrate stable bipedal walking
- **2000**: Honda's ASIMO became famous for its advanced mobility and human interaction capabilities

## Recent Developments

The 21st century has seen rapid advancement in humanoid robotics:

- **2010s**: Boston Dynamics' Atlas robot demonstrated remarkable mobility and balance
- **2020s**: Tesla's Optimus and other companies' humanoid robots show increasing commercial interest
- **Current**: Multiple companies developing humanoid robots for various applications
