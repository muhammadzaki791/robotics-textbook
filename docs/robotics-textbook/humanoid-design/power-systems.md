# Power Systems and Management

## Battery Technologies

**Lithium-ion Batteries**: Most common for humanoid robots.
- Advantages: High energy density, rechargeable, mature technology
- Disadvantages: Safety concerns, degradation over time, cost

**Battery Management**:
- **Voltage Regulation**: Stable power for sensitive electronics
- **Current Monitoring**: Prevent over-discharge and overheating
- **Thermal Management**: Cooling systems for safe operation
- **Charge Control**: Intelligent charging for battery life

## Power Consumption Considerations

**Standby Power**: Power consumed when robot is idle but ready.

**Locomotion Power**: Energy required for walking and balance maintenance.

**Manipulation Power**: Energy for arm and hand movements.

**Processing Power**: Energy for computation and control systems.

**Communication Power**: Energy for wireless communication and displays.

## Energy Efficiency Strategies

**Efficient Gaits**: Optimizing walking patterns for minimal energy consumption.

**Sleep Modes**: Reducing power consumption during inactivity.

**Regenerative Systems**: Capturing energy during braking or descent.

**Optimized Control**: Minimizing unnecessary movements and control effort.

## Power Distribution

**Voltage Levels**: Different components may require different voltage levels.

**Power Regulation**: Clean, stable power for sensitive systems.

**Redundancy**: Backup power systems for critical functions.

**Cable Management**: Efficient routing of power cables throughout the robot.
