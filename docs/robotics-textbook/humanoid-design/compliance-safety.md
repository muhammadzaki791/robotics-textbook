# Compliance and Safety

## Mechanical Compliance

**Passive Compliance**: Mechanical design that allows safe interaction.

**Active Compliance**: Control systems that adjust robot impedance.

**Impact Absorption**: Design features that absorb collision energy.

## Safety Protocols

**Emergency Stop**: Reliable systems for immediate robot stopping.

**Force Limiting**: Mechanical and control-based force limiting.

**Collision Detection**: Systems to detect and respond to unintended contact.

**Safe Failure Modes**: Ensuring robot remains safe when systems fail.

## Human Safety Considerations

**Contact Forces**: Limiting forces during human-robot interaction.

**Sharp Edges**: Rounded edges and smooth surfaces to prevent injury.

**Pinch Points**: Eliminating areas where humans can be caught.

**Chemical Safety**: Safe materials and fluids that may contact humans.

## Design Trade-offs and Optimization

### Performance vs. Safety

**Precision vs. Compliance**: Trade-offs between precise control and safe interaction.

**Speed vs. Safety**: Faster motion may be less safe for human interaction.

**Power vs. Safety**: Higher power actuators may be more dangerous.

### Cost vs. Capability

**Research vs. Commercial**: Different design priorities for research vs. commercial robots.

**Component Selection**: Balancing performance and cost of individual components.

**Manufacturability**: Designing for efficient production and assembly.

### Weight vs. Strength

**Materials**: Choosing materials that optimize the weight-strength trade-off.

**Structural Design**: Using structural principles to maximize strength while minimizing weight.

**Component Integration**: Combining functions to reduce overall component count.
