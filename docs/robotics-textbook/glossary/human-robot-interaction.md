# Human-Robot Interaction

**Human-Robot Interaction (HRI)**: The study of interactions between humans and robots, including communication, collaboration, and social behavior.

**Social Robot**: A robot designed to interact with humans in a socially acceptable manner, often featuring human-like characteristics and social behaviors.

**Natural Language Processing (NLP)**: A field of AI focused on enabling computers to understand, interpret, and generate human language, important for human-robot communication.

**Proactive Behavior**: Behavior where the robot anticipates future needs or events and acts accordingly, rather than simply reacting to current situations.

**Reactive Behavior**: Behavior where the robot responds directly to current sensory input, without planning or prediction of future states.

**Shared Cognitive Load**: Distributing tasks between human and robot cognition, such as attention, memory, and decision making.

**Trust**: The confidence humans have in robot capabilities and behavior, critical for effective human-robot collaboration.

**Social Integration**: The process of robots becoming accepted members of human communities and social structures.

**Cultural Adaptation**: The ability of robots to adapt to human cultures, including learning cultural norms and practices.

**Extended Cognition**: The concept of robots as cognitive extensions of humans, providing memory augmentation, processing power, and enhanced sensing capabilities.

**Human-Robot Symbiosis**: The seamless integration of humans and robots working as integrated teams with predictive assistance and shared memory.

**Asimov's Laws of Robotics**: A set of fictional rules devised by science fiction author Isaac Asimov, intended to prevent robots from harming humans.

**Robot Rights**: Questions about the moral status and potential rights of advanced robots in society.

**Ethical Frameworks**: Systems of moral principles that guide the development and use of humanoid robots, including deontological, consequentialist, and virtue ethics approaches.

**Privacy**: The protection of personal information and data collected by robots during human interaction.

**Safety Protocols**: Procedures and systems designed to ensure safe interaction between humans and robots.

**Affective Computing**: Computing that relates to, arises from, or influences emotions, important for robots that need to recognize and respond to human emotions.

**Anthropomorphism**: The attribution of human characteristics or behavior to non-human entities, such as robots. In robotics, it can refer to designing robots with human-like features or behaviors.

**Appropriate Response**: The ability of a robot to respond in a manner that is socially acceptable and contextually appropriate during human-robot interaction.

**Attention Detection**: The ability of a robot to determine where a human is focusing their attention, often using gaze tracking or other sensors.

**Autonomous Interaction**: Interaction between robots and humans where the robot makes decisions and takes actions without explicit human commands.

**Behavioral Adaptation**: The ability of robots to modify their behavior based on feedback from human users or environmental context.

**Collaborative Robotics**: The design and use of robots that work together with humans in shared workspaces, emphasizing safety and cooperation.

**Communication Channel**: The medium through which humans and robots exchange information, including verbal, non-verbal, and digital channels.

**Context Awareness**: The ability of a robot to understand and respond to the situational context of interactions, including time, location, and social setting.

**Cooperative Behavior**: Actions taken by robots that benefit the human-robot team rather than just the robot's individual objectives.

**Deictic Gestures**: Gestures that point to specific objects or locations, important for robots to understand and use during communication.

**Distributed Cognition**: The idea that cognitive processes are distributed across humans, robots, and the environment in human-robot teams.

**Emotional Intelligence**: The ability to recognize, understand, and manage emotions, both one's own and others', important for social robots.

**Emotion Recognition**: The ability of robots to identify human emotions through facial expressions, voice tone, body language, or physiological signals.

**Embodied Interaction**: Interaction that involves the physical presence and actions of both humans and robots in shared space.

**Ethical AI**: Artificial intelligence systems designed and operated according to ethical principles, ensuring fair, transparent, and responsible behavior.

**Explainable AI (XAI)**: AI systems that can explain their reasoning and decision-making processes in human-understandable terms, important for trust and acceptance.

**Face-to-Face Interaction**: Direct interaction between humans and robots in the same physical space, requiring appropriate social behaviors.

**Gaze Following**: The ability of robots to follow human gaze direction, important for joint attention and social interaction.

**Gestural Communication**: Communication through body movements, hand gestures, and facial expressions between humans and robots.

**Haptic Communication**: Communication through touch and physical contact between humans and robots.

**Human-Agent Teaming**: The collaboration between humans and artificial agents (including robots) in shared tasks and decision-making.

**Human-Centered Design**: An approach to robot design that prioritizes human needs, capabilities, and preferences in the development process.

**Human-Robot Collaboration**: A form of interaction where humans and robots work together to achieve common goals, with complementary roles and responsibilities.

**Implicit Communication**: Communication that occurs without explicit verbal or gestural signals, such as through context or shared understanding.

**Intention Recognition**: The ability of robots to infer human intentions from observed behavior, gestures, or other cues.

**Joint Attention**: A state where a human and robot focus on the same object or event, fundamental to collaborative interaction.

**Mental Model**: The internal representation that humans or robots have of the other agent's capabilities, intentions, and likely behavior.

**Mutual Adaptation**: The process where both humans and robots adjust their behavior to improve collaboration and interaction quality.

**Non-Verbal Communication**: Communication through body language, facial expressions, gestures, and other non-verbal cues between humans and robots.

**Normative Behavior**: Behavior that conforms to social norms and expectations, important for robots to be accepted in human environments.

**Paralinguistic Features**: Vocal features such as pitch, tone, and rhythm that convey meaning beyond the words themselves, important for natural interaction.

**Personal Space**: The physical space around a person that they consider psychologically theirs, which robots must respect during interaction.

**Proxemics**: The study of how humans and robots use space in interaction, including distances, orientation, and spatial relationships.

**Reciprocal Interaction**: Interaction where both humans and robots actively contribute and respond to each other, rather than one-way communication.

**Robot Anthropomorphism**: The tendency of humans to attribute human characteristics to robots, which can affect interaction and expectations.

**Robot Etiquette**: The social rules and conventions that govern appropriate robot behavior in human environments.

**Robot Personality**: The consistent behavioral patterns that make a robot appear to have a distinct character or persona.

**Situational Awareness**: The perception of environmental elements and events with respect to time and space, crucial for appropriate robot responses.

**Social Cues**: Signals that convey social information, such as eye contact, body posture, and facial expressions, important for natural interaction.

**Social Navigation**: The ability of robots to move through human spaces while respecting social norms and avoiding disruption.

**Social Presence**: The degree to which a robot is perceived as a real social actor rather than just a machine.

**Social Robotics**: A field of robotics that focuses on creating robots that can interact with humans in socially meaningful ways.

**Socio-Emotional Behavior**: Robot behaviors that address human social and emotional needs during interaction.

**Synchronous Interaction**: Interaction where humans and robots engage simultaneously in real-time, as opposed to asynchronous communication.

**Turn-Taking**: The social mechanism for managing conversation and interaction flow between humans and robots.

**User Experience (UX)**: The overall experience of a person using a robot system, including emotions, beliefs, preferences, and responses.

**User Trust**: The confidence that users have in a robot's reliability, safety, and ability to perform as expected.

**Verbal Communication**: Communication through spoken language between humans and robots, requiring speech recognition and synthesis.

**Voice Interaction**: Communication through spoken language, including the robot's ability to recognize speech and generate appropriate responses.

**Vulnerability**: The degree to which humans are willing to rely on and be affected by robot actions, related to trust and safety.

**Zero-Order Intentionality**: The ability of robots to understand that humans have beliefs, desires, and intentions that may differ from their own.
