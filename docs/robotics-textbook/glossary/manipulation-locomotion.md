# Manipulation and Locomotion

**Bipedal Locomotion**: The act of walking on two legs, the primary form of locomotion for humanoid robots.

**Legged Locomotion**: The act of moving using legs, as opposed to wheels or tracks, requiring sophisticated balance and control.

**Manipulation**: The process of using a robot's appendages to modify the position, orientation, or state of objects in the environment.

**Dexterous Manipulation**: The skillful handling of objects using robotic hands or end-effectors, typically involving fine motor control and precise positioning.

**Grasp Planning**: The process of determining how a robot should position and configure its hand to securely grasp an object.

**Friction Cone**: A geometric representation of the range of forces that can be applied at a contact point without causing slip, defined by the coefficient of friction.

**Gait**: The pattern of leg movements used by a walking robot, including parameters such as step length, step width, and step timing.

**Haptic Feedback**: The use of touch and motion to communicate information, important for safe and effective human-robot interaction.

**Visual Servoing**: The control of robot motion based on visual feedback, using cameras to guide robot movements.

**Trajectory Optimization**: The process of finding the optimal path for a robot to follow, considering factors such as time, energy, and safety.

**Path Planning**: The process of determining a sequence of movements to move from a start position to a goal position while avoiding obstacles.

**Manipulability**: A measure of how easily a robot can move its end-effector in different directions, important for evaluating robot design and task planning.

**Underactuated System**: A mechanical system with fewer actuators than degrees of freedom, common in robotics and requiring special control approaches.

**Overactuated Robot**: A robot with more actuators than degrees of freedom, providing redundancy that can be used for optimization of secondary objectives.

**Anthropomorphism**: The attribution of human characteristics or behavior to non-human entities, such as robots. In robotics, it can refer to designing robots with human-like features or behaviors.

**Biomimicry**: The design of robots inspired by biological systems and processes, aiming to replicate the efficiency and adaptability found in nature.

**Adaptive Gait**: A walking pattern that changes in response to terrain conditions, obstacles, or task requirements, allowing robots to navigate diverse environments.

**Ankle Strategy**: A balance control technique that uses ankle joint movements to maintain balance by shifting the center of pressure.

**Anti-Slip Grasp**: A grasp configuration that prevents objects from slipping during manipulation by applying appropriate forces and contact points.

**Asymmetric Gait**: A walking pattern where the left and right leg movements differ, useful for turning or navigating uneven terrain.

**Balance Recovery**: The process of returning to a stable state after a disturbance that threatens to cause a fall.

**Bipedal Walking**: A form of locomotion where the robot moves on two legs, requiring continuous balance control and coordination.

**Cartesian Impedance Control**: A control method that specifies the mechanical impedance of the robot's end-effector in Cartesian space.

**Center of Mass (CoM)**: The point where the robot's mass is concentrated, crucial for balance and locomotion control.

**Center of Pressure (CoP)**: The point where the ground reaction force acts, important for balance assessment and control.

**Contact Stability**: The ability of a robot to maintain stable contact with the environment during manipulation or locomotion tasks.

**Crawling Gait**: A low-profile locomotion mode where the robot moves close to the ground, useful for navigating under obstacles.

**Cyclic Gait**: A repetitive walking pattern that forms the basis of stable bipedal locomotion.

**Dance-like Motion**: Rhythmic, coordinated movements that can be used for locomotion or manipulation tasks requiring fluid motion.

**Dynamic Balance**: The ability to maintain balance while in motion, requiring continuous adjustment of the center of mass and support base.

**Dynamic Grasping**: Grasping objects while in motion, such as catching a moving object or grasping during walking.

**Dynamic Walking**: Walking that relies on dynamic stability rather than static stability, allowing for more human-like gait patterns.

**End-Effector Trajectory**: The path followed by the robot's end-effector during manipulation tasks.

**Environment Interaction**: The process of sensing and responding to environmental conditions during locomotion or manipulation.

**Exoskeleton Control**: Control strategies for wearable robotic devices that assist human locomotion or manipulation.

**Foot Placement Control**: The precise control of where the feet make contact with the ground during walking.

**Foot Trajectory Planning**: The planning of the path that the robot's feet follow during locomotion.

**Force Control**: Control of the forces applied by the robot during interaction with objects or the environment.

**Force Closure**: A grasp configuration where the contact forces can resist any external wrench applied to the object.

**Gait Adaptation**: The ability to modify walking patterns in response to environmental changes or disturbances.

**Gait Cycle**: The complete sequence of movements from one foot contact to the next contact of the same foot.

**Gait Phase**: A specific stage within the walking cycle, such as stance phase or swing phase.

**Gait Planning**: The process of determining the sequence of foot placements and body movements for locomotion.

**Gait Transition**: The change from one walking pattern to another, such as from slow to fast walking.

**Grasp Configuration**: The specific positioning and orientation of a robot's fingers or end-effector during grasping.

**Grasp Force Optimization**: The process of determining the optimal forces to apply during grasping to ensure stability while avoiding damage.

**Grasp Synthesis**: The computational process of determining feasible grasp configurations for a given object.

**Hand Configuration**: The specific joint angles and positions of a robotic hand during manipulation tasks.

**Hip Strategy**: A balance control technique that uses hip joint movements to maintain balance by shifting the center of mass.

**Human-Like Walking**: Walking patterns that closely mimic human gait characteristics, including step length, timing, and joint coordination.

**Impedance Control**: A control strategy that makes the robot behave like a spring-damper system during interaction.

**Inverse Kinematics for Manipulation**: The calculation of joint angles required to position the robot's end-effector for manipulation tasks.

**Locomotion Planning**: The process of determining a sequence of movements to move the robot from one location to another.

**Manipulation Planning**: The process of determining a sequence of movements to manipulate objects in the environment.

**Multi-Contact Manipulation**: Manipulation tasks that involve multiple contact points with the object or environment.

**Multi-Legged Locomotion**: Locomotion using more than two legs, such as quadrupedal or hexapodal walking.

**Multi-Step Planning**: Planning that considers multiple future steps in manipulation or locomotion tasks.

**Obstacle Avoidance**: The ability to navigate around obstacles during locomotion or manipulation tasks.

**Omnidirectional Walking**: Walking in any direction without changing the robot's body orientation.

**Operational Space Control**: Control of robot tasks in their natural coordinate systems, such as end-effector position.

**Over-Grip Grasp**: A grasp where the fingers wrap around an object, typically providing secure holding.

**Pincer Grasp**: A grasp using thumb and finger opposition, similar to human precision grip.

**Point Foot**: A simplified model of a foot with a single point contact with the ground.

**Prosthetic Control**: Control strategies for robotic prosthetic devices that assist human locomotion or manipulation.

**Quadrupedal Locomotion**: Locomotion using four legs, offering stability advantages over bipedal locomotion.

**Rapidly-exploring Random Tree (RRT)**: A path planning algorithm that builds a tree of possible paths by randomly exploring the space.

**Rhythmic Movement**: Periodic motions that can be used for locomotion or manipulation tasks requiring repetitive motion.

**Rolling Gait**: A locomotion mode where the robot moves by rolling, useful for certain terrain types.

**Scuffing**: The dragging of feet during walking, which can cause instability in bipedal robots.

**Semi-Forced Walking**: A walking mode where the robot uses active control to move the swing leg forward.

**Singularity Avoidance**: The process of avoiding configurations where the robot loses degrees of freedom during manipulation.

**Singularity Robust Control**: Control strategies that maintain performance near singular configurations.

**Slip Detection**: The ability to detect when an object is beginning to slip from the robot's grasp.

**Slip Prevention**: Techniques to prevent objects from slipping during manipulation tasks.

**Slope Walking**: Walking on inclined surfaces, requiring adjustments to balance and gait parameters.

**Squatting**: A low posture where the robot bends its legs, useful for stability or reaching low objects.

**Stance Phase**: The phase of walking when the foot is in contact with the ground.

**Static Balance**: Balance maintained without motion, typically requiring the center of mass to be within the support polygon.

**Static Grasp**: A grasp that holds an object without motion, relying on friction and contact forces.

**Step Climbing**: The ability to walk up or down steps or stairs, requiring precise foot placement.

**Step Timing**: The precise timing of foot placement during walking, critical for stability.

**Stiffness Control**: Control of the mechanical stiffness of the robot's joints or end-effector.

**Stumble Recovery**: The process of recovering from a stumble or near-fall during locomotion.

**Swing Phase**: The phase of walking when the foot is off the ground and moving forward.

**Synchronized Motion**: Coordinated movements of multiple robot parts for complex manipulation or locomotion.

**Tactile Feedback**: Sensory information obtained through touch, important for manipulation and locomotion control.

**Terrain Adaptation**: The ability to adjust locomotion patterns based on ground conditions.

**Three-Point Contact**: A stable configuration where the robot maintains contact with the environment at three points.

**Torque Control**: Control of the rotational forces applied by the robot's joints.

**Trajectory Following**: The ability to follow a planned path with specified timing and accuracy.

**Under-Grip Grasp**: A grasp where the fingers support the object from below, often used for lifting.

**Variable Impedance**: The ability to change the mechanical impedance of robot joints during tasks.

**Visual-Guided Manipulation**: Manipulation tasks that use visual feedback for precise positioning and control.

**Walking Stability**: The ability to maintain balance during the walking process, preventing falls.

**Whole-Body Control**: Control strategies that coordinate all of the robot's joints for manipulation or locomotion tasks.

**Wrench**: A 6-dimensional vector representing forces and torques applied to an object, used in grasp analysis.
