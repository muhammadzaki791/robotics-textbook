# Ethical Frameworks for Humanoid Robotics

## Traditional Ethical Approaches

**Deontological Ethics**: Based on rules and duties. In robotics, this might translate to absolute rules like "robots must not harm humans."

**Consequentialism**: Actions are judged by their outcomes. A robot's behavior is evaluated based on the consequences of its actions.

**Virtue Ethics**: Focuses on character and moral virtues. How can robots embody beneficial characteristics?

## Robot Ethics Specific Frameworks

**Asimov's Laws of Robotics**: Though fictional, these laws have influenced real discussions:
1. A robot may not injure a human being or allow a human being to come to harm
2. A robot must obey the orders given to it by human beings, except where such orders would conflict with the First Law
3. A robot must protect its own existence as long as such protection does not conflict with the First or Second Laws

**Limitations**: These laws face logical paradoxes and don't address modern robotics complexities.

## Contemporary Robot Ethics

**Machine Ethics**: Programming ethical decision-making into robots for situations requiring moral judgment.

**Roboethics**: The study of how humans should behave toward robots and how robots should behave toward humans.

**Human-Robot Interaction Ethics**: Focused specifically on the ethical dimensions of human-robot relationships.

## Rights and Personhood

**Robot Rights**: Should robots have rights as they become more sophisticated?
- Currently: Robots are property, not rights-bearing entities
- Future considerations: Advanced AI systems may raise questions about moral status

**Human Rights**: How do humanoid robots affect human rights and dignity?
- Right to human interaction
- Right to privacy
- Right to employment
