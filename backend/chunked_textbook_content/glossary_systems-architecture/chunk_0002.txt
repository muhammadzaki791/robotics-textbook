**Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)**: Hypothetical AI that possesses the general intelligence of a human being, with the ability to understand or learn any intellectual task that a human being can. **Actuator Saturation**: The condition where an actuator reaches its maximum or minimum output limit, which can affect control performance and stability. **Adaptive Architecture**: A system architecture that can modify its structure or behavior in response to changing conditions or requirements. **Anomaly Detection**: The process of identifying unusual patterns or behaviors that do not conform to expected norms, important for robot health monitoring. **API (Application Programming Interface)**: A set of rules and protocols for building and interacting with software applications, crucial for robot software integration. **Asynchronous Communication**: Communication between system components that does not require simultaneous participation, allowing for more flexible robot architectures. **Autonomous Navigation**: The ability of a robot to move through an environment without human intervention, using sensors and planning algorithms. **Bayesian Network**: A probabilistic graphical model that represents a set of variables and their conditional dependencies, used for robot decision making under uncertainty. **Behavior Tree**: A hierarchical structure used to organize and execute robot behaviors, providing a flexible and modular approach to robot control. **Bootstrapping**: The process of initializing a robot system from a minimal starting state, including sensor calibration and system diagnostics. **Calibration**: The process of adjusting a sensor or system to ensure accurate measurements, critical for robot perception and control. **Cloud Robotics**: The use of cloud computing to provide robots with additional computational power, data storage, and shared knowledge. **Cognitive Architecture**: The structural organization of robot intelligence systems, defining how different cognitive components interact and process information. **Command Interface**: The system through which humans or other systems communicate instructions to a robot, ranging from high-level task specifications to low-level motor commands. **Computational Graph**: A directed graph where nodes represent mathematical operations and edges represent data flow, used in modern robot learning systems. **Computational Load**: The amount of processing power required by robot algorithms, important for real-time performance and system design. **Configuration Management**: The process of managing and tracking changes to robot software and hardware configurations over time. **Context Awareness**: The ability of a robot system to understand and respond to environmental and situational context. **Control Architecture**: The organizational structure of a robot's control system, including the hierarchy and interaction of different control layers.
