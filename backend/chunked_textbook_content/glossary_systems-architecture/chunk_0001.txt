

# Systems and Architecture

**Robotics Operating System (ROS)**: Flexible framework for writing robot software, providing services designed for a heterogeneous computer cluster such as hardware abstraction, device drivers, libraries, visualizers, message-passing, and package management. **Distributed Intelligence**: Intelligence that emerges from the interaction of multiple simple agents, as opposed to centralized intelligence. **Swarm Robotics**: The coordination of multiple robots working together to achieve collective behavior and accomplish tasks. **Whole-Body Control**: A control approach that coordinates all of a robot's degrees of freedom simultaneously to achieve multiple objectives such as balance, manipulation, and obstacle avoidance. **Sim-to-Real Transfer**: The process of transferring policies or models trained in simulation to real robotic systems, addressing the reality gap between simulation and reality. **Long-Term Autonomy**: The ability of robots to operate independently for extended periods, handling various challenges and maintaining performance over time. **General-Purpose Robot**: A robot capable of performing a wide variety of tasks rather than being specialized for specific functions. **Safety-Critical System**: A system whose failure could result in loss of life, significant property damage, or environmental harm, requiring special attention to safety in design and operation. **Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU)**: A device that measures and reports a body's specific force, angular rate, and sometimes the magnetic field surrounding the body, used for navigation and balance control. **Optimization**: The process of making a system or design as effective or functional as possible, fundamental to many robotics problems including motion planning and control. **Heuristic**: A practical approach to problem-solving that may not guarantee optimal results but is sufficient for reaching immediate goals, often used in robot planning. **Telerobotics**: The remote control of robots, allowing human operators to control robots from a distance. **Episodic Memory**: Memory of specific events and experiences, increasingly important in robots that need to remember and learn from specific interactions. **Grounded Language Learning**: The process of learning language through physical interaction with the environment, important for robots that need to understand human language in context. **Non-Holonomic Constraint**: A constraint on a robot's motion that cannot be integrated to form a constraint on position alone, common in wheeled robots and affecting motion planning. **Utility Function**: A function that assigns a numerical value to different states or outcomes, representing the desirability of each state in decision-making processes.
