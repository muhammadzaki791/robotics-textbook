

**Gait Cycle**: The complete sequence of movements from one foot contact to the next contact of the same foot. **Gait Phase**: A specific stage within the walking cycle, such as stance phase or swing phase. **Gait Planning**: The process of determining the sequence of foot placements and body movements for locomotion. **Gait Transition**: The change from one walking pattern to another, such as from slow to fast walking. **Grasp Configuration**: The specific positioning and orientation of a robot's fingers or end-effector during grasping. **Grasp Force Optimization**: The process of determining the optimal forces to apply during grasping to ensure stability while avoiding damage. **Grasp Synthesis**: The computational process of determining feasible grasp configurations for a given object. **Hand Configuration**: The specific joint angles and positions of a robotic hand during manipulation tasks. **Hip Strategy**: A balance control technique that uses hip joint movements to maintain balance by shifting the center of mass. **Human-Like Walking**: Walking patterns that closely mimic human gait characteristics, including step length, timing, and joint coordination. **Impedance Control**: A control strategy that makes the robot behave like a spring-damper system during interaction. **Inverse Kinematics for Manipulation**: The calculation of joint angles required to position the robot's end-effector for manipulation tasks. **Locomotion Planning**: The process of determining a sequence of movements to move the robot from one location to another. **Manipulation Planning**: The process of determining a sequence of movements to manipulate objects in the environment. **Multi-Contact Manipulation**: Manipulation tasks that involve multiple contact points with the object or environment. **Multi-Legged Locomotion**: Locomotion using more than two legs, such as quadrupedal or hexapodal walking. **Multi-Step Planning**: Planning that considers multiple future steps in manipulation or locomotion tasks. **Obstacle Avoidance**: The ability to navigate around obstacles during locomotion or manipulation tasks. **Omnidirectional Walking**: Walking in any direction without changing the robot's body orientation. **Operational Space Control**: Control of robot tasks in their natural coordinate systems, such as end-effector position. **Over-Grip Grasp**: A grasp where the fingers wrap around an object, typically providing secure holding. **Pincer Grasp**: A grasp using thumb and finger opposition, similar to human precision grip. **Point Foot**: A simplified model of a foot with a single point contact with the ground.
