

# Manipulation and Locomotion

**Bipedal Locomotion**: The act of walking on two legs, the primary form of locomotion for humanoid robots. **Legged Locomotion**: The act of moving using legs, as opposed to wheels or tracks, requiring sophisticated balance and control. **Manipulation**: The process of using a robot's appendages to modify the position, orientation, or state of objects in the environment. **Dexterous Manipulation**: The skillful handling of objects using robotic hands or end-effectors, typically involving fine motor control and precise positioning. **Grasp Planning**: The process of determining how a robot should position and configure its hand to securely grasp an object. **Friction Cone**: A geometric representation of the range of forces that can be applied at a contact point without causing slip, defined by the coefficient of friction. **Gait**: The pattern of leg movements used by a walking robot, including parameters such as step length, step width, and step timing. **Haptic Feedback**: The use of touch and motion to communicate information, important for safe and effective human-robot interaction. **Visual Servoing**: The control of robot motion based on visual feedback, using cameras to guide robot movements. **Trajectory Optimization**: The process of finding the optimal path for a robot to follow, considering factors such as time, energy, and safety. **Path Planning**: The process of determining a sequence of movements to move from a start position to a goal position while avoiding obstacles. **Manipulability**: A measure of how easily a robot can move its end-effector in different directions, important for evaluating robot design and task planning. **Underactuated System**: A mechanical system with fewer actuators than degrees of freedom, common in robotics and requiring special control approaches. **Overactuated Robot**: A robot with more actuators than degrees of freedom, providing redundancy that can be used for optimization of secondary objectives. **Anthropomorphism**: The attribution of human characteristics or behavior to non-human entities, such as robots. In robotics, it can refer to designing robots with human-like features or behaviors. **Biomimicry**: The design of robots inspired by biological systems and processes, aiming to replicate the efficiency and adaptability found in nature. **Adaptive Gait**: A walking pattern that changes in response to terrain conditions, obstacles, or task requirements, allowing robots to navigate diverse environments.
