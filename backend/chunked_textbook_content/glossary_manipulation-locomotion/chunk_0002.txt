**Ankle Strategy**: A balance control technique that uses ankle joint movements to maintain balance by shifting the center of pressure. **Anti-Slip Grasp**: A grasp configuration that prevents objects from slipping during manipulation by applying appropriate forces and contact points. **Asymmetric Gait**: A walking pattern where the left and right leg movements differ, useful for turning or navigating uneven terrain. **Balance Recovery**: The process of returning to a stable state after a disturbance that threatens to cause a fall. **Bipedal Walking**: A form of locomotion where the robot moves on two legs, requiring continuous balance control and coordination. **Cartesian Impedance Control**: A control method that specifies the mechanical impedance of the robot's end-effector in Cartesian space. **Center of Mass (CoM)**: The point where the robot's mass is concentrated, crucial for balance and locomotion control. **Center of Pressure (CoP)**: The point where the ground reaction force acts, important for balance assessment and control. **Contact Stability**: The ability of a robot to maintain stable contact with the environment during manipulation or locomotion tasks. **Crawling Gait**: A low-profile locomotion mode where the robot moves close to the ground, useful for navigating under obstacles. **Cyclic Gait**: A repetitive walking pattern that forms the basis of stable bipedal locomotion. **Dance-like Motion**: Rhythmic, coordinated movements that can be used for locomotion or manipulation tasks requiring fluid motion. **Dynamic Balance**: The ability to maintain balance while in motion, requiring continuous adjustment of the center of mass and support base. **Dynamic Grasping**: Grasping objects while in motion, such as catching a moving object or grasping during walking. **Dynamic Walking**: Walking that relies on dynamic stability rather than static stability, allowing for more human-like gait patterns. **End-Effector Trajectory**: The path followed by the robot's end-effector during manipulation tasks. **Environment Interaction**: The process of sensing and responding to environmental conditions during locomotion or manipulation. **Exoskeleton Control**: Control strategies for wearable robotic devices that assist human locomotion or manipulation. **Foot Placement Control**: The precise control of where the feet make contact with the ground during walking. **Foot Trajectory Planning**: The planning of the path that the robot's feet follow during locomotion. **Force Control**: Control of the forces applied by the robot during interaction with objects or the environment.
