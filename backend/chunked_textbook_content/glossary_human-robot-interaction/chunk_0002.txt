**Attention Detection**: The ability of a robot to determine where a human is focusing their attention, often using gaze tracking or other sensors. **Autonomous Interaction**: Interaction between robots and humans where the robot makes decisions and takes actions without explicit human commands. **Behavioral Adaptation**: The ability of robots to modify their behavior based on feedback from human users or environmental context. **Collaborative Robotics**: The design and use of robots that work together with humans in shared workspaces, emphasizing safety and cooperation. **Communication Channel**: The medium through which humans and robots exchange information, including verbal, non-verbal, and digital channels. **Context Awareness**: The ability of a robot to understand and respond to the situational context of interactions, including time, location, and social setting. **Cooperative Behavior**: Actions taken by robots that benefit the human-robot team rather than just the robot's individual objectives. **Deictic Gestures**: Gestures that point to specific objects or locations, important for robots to understand and use during communication. **Distributed Cognition**: The idea that cognitive processes are distributed across humans, robots, and the environment in human-robot teams. **Emotional Intelligence**: The ability to recognize, understand, and manage emotions, both one's own and others', important for social robots. **Emotion Recognition**: The ability of robots to identify human emotions through facial expressions, voice tone, body language, or physiological signals. **Embodied Interaction**: Interaction that involves the physical presence and actions of both humans and robots in shared space. **Ethical AI**: Artificial intelligence systems designed and operated according to ethical principles, ensuring fair, transparent, and responsible behavior. **Explainable AI (XAI)**: AI systems that can explain their reasoning and decision-making processes in human-understandable terms, important for trust and acceptance. **Face-to-Face Interaction**: Direct interaction between humans and robots in the same physical space, requiring appropriate social behaviors. **Gaze Following**: The ability of robots to follow human gaze direction, important for joint attention and social interaction. **Gestural Communication**: Communication through body movements, hand gestures, and facial expressions between humans and robots. **Haptic Communication**: Communication through touch and physical contact between humans and robots. **Human-Agent Teaming**: The collaboration between humans and artificial agents (including robots) in shared tasks and decision-making. **Human-Centered Design**: An approach to robot design that prioritizes human needs, capabilities, and preferences in the development process.
