

# Human-Robot Interaction

**Human-Robot Interaction (HRI)**: The study of interactions between humans and robots, including communication, collaboration, and social behavior. **Social Robot**: A robot designed to interact with humans in a socially acceptable manner, often featuring human-like characteristics and social behaviors. **Natural Language Processing (NLP)**: A field of AI focused on enabling computers to understand, interpret, and generate human language, important for human-robot communication. **Proactive Behavior**: Behavior where the robot anticipates future needs or events and acts accordingly, rather than simply reacting to current situations. **Reactive Behavior**: Behavior where the robot responds directly to current sensory input, without planning or prediction of future states. **Shared Cognitive Load**: Distributing tasks between human and robot cognition, such as attention, memory, and decision making. **Trust**: The confidence humans have in robot capabilities and behavior, critical for effective human-robot collaboration. **Social Integration**: The process of robots becoming accepted members of human communities and social structures. **Cultural Adaptation**: The ability of robots to adapt to human cultures, including learning cultural norms and practices. **Extended Cognition**: The concept of robots as cognitive extensions of humans, providing memory augmentation, processing power, and enhanced sensing capabilities. **Human-Robot Symbiosis**: The seamless integration of humans and robots working as integrated teams with predictive assistance and shared memory. **Asimov's Laws of Robotics**: A set of fictional rules devised by science fiction author Isaac Asimov, intended to prevent robots from harming humans. **Robot Rights**: Questions about the moral status and potential rights of advanced robots in society. **Ethical Frameworks**: Systems of moral principles that guide the development and use of humanoid robots, including deontological, consequentialist, and virtue ethics approaches. **Privacy**: The protection of personal information and data collected by robots during human interaction. **Safety Protocols**: Procedures and systems designed to ensure safe interaction between humans and robots. **Affective Computing**: Computing that relates to, arises from, or influences emotions, important for robots that need to recognize and respond to human emotions. **Anthropomorphism**: The attribution of human characteristics or behavior to non-human entities, such as robots. In robotics, it can refer to designing robots with human-like features or behaviors. **Appropriate Response**: The ability of a robot to respond in a manner that is socially acceptable and contextually appropriate during human-robot interaction.
