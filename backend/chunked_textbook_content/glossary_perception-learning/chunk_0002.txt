**Gaussian Process**: A probabilistic machine learning model that can represent uncertainty in predictions, useful for robot learning and control. **Q-Learning**: A model-free reinforcement learning algorithm that learns a policy telling an agent what action to take under what circumstances. **Markov Decision Process (MDP)**: A mathematical framework for modeling decision-making in situations where outcomes are partly random and partly under the control of a decision maker, fundamental to reinforcement learning. **Explainable AI**: AI systems that can explain their reasoning and decision-making processes in human-understandable terms. **Black Box Problem**: The challenge of understanding how an AI system makes decisions when the internal processes are opaque or difficult to interpret. **Active Learning**: A machine learning paradigm where the algorithm can query a user or an oracle to obtain new data points, useful for efficient robot learning. **Actor-Critic Method**: A reinforcement learning approach that combines value-based and policy-based methods, with an actor that learns the policy and a critic that evaluates it. **Affordance**: The possibility of an action that an object offers to an agent; for example, a handle affords grasping. **Augmented Reality (AR)**: A technology that overlays digital information onto the real world, increasingly used for robot programming and human-robot interaction. **Autonomous Learning**: The ability of robots to learn and improve their performance without human intervention. **Bayesian Inference**: A statistical method that updates the probability of a hypothesis as more evidence becomes available, used in robot perception and decision making. **Bayesian Optimization**: A sequential optimization technique that uses Bayesian inference to find the minimum or maximum of an objective function, useful for robot learning. **Biomimetic Learning**: Learning approaches that mimic biological systems, particularly useful for robot locomotion and control. **Calibration**: The process of adjusting a sensor or system to ensure accurate measurements, critical for robot perception. **Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA)**: A statistical method to find correlations between two sets of variables, useful for multi-modal sensor fusion. **Clustering**: An unsupervised learning technique that groups similar data points together, used for scene understanding and object recognition. **Convolution**: A mathematical operation that combines two functions to produce a third, fundamental to CNNs and signal processing in robotics. **Cross-Validation**: A technique for assessing how well a machine learning model will generalize to new data, important for robot learning.
